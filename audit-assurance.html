Why do UAE companies need an audit report?

Most free zones ask for audited financial statements with the annual licence renewal. JAFZA, DMCC and DAFZA ask every licensed company for them, including companies with little or no activity. Types of audit

What types of audit can you get through Modest?

There are five kinds: statutory, internal, compliance, due diligence and special purpose. Statutory is the one free zones and Corporate Tax rules usually refer to. The other four cover control reviews, deals and one-off requests.

01

Statutory audit

The yearly audit of your financial statements, signed by a registered auditor. JAFZA, DMCC and DAFZA ask for it with the annual renewal. Other zones and the Corporate Tax rules have their own conditions, and we check yours first.

02

Internal audit

A review of how your company handles money: approvals, cash, purchasing, payroll and reporting. It suits owners who want an outside check on controls, even where no authority asks for one.

03

Compliance audit

A check of your records against specific rules, such as VAT, Corporate Tax or your free zone's conditions. It helps before a tax review, a licence renewal or a change of ownership.

04

Due diligence audit

A focused financial review for a deal: a buyer checking a target company, an investor assessing a business, or partners agreeing terms. It looks at specific risks, not at a full year.

05

Special purpose audit

An audit prepared for one named purpose, such as a bank facility, a tender, a court matter or an application. The scope and format follow what the recipient asks for.

Documents required

What documents do you need for an audit in UAE?

For most audits you need your trade licence, MOA, a full year of bank statements, a trial balance, the general ledger and your sales and purchase listings. We confirm the exact list once we know your company type and why you need the audit.

Standard documents:

  • Trade licence copy
  • MOA, memorandum of association
  • Bank statements, full financial year, all accounts
  • Trial balance, as at financial year end
  • General ledger, all transactions for the year
  • Sales and purchase invoices, complete listing
  • Fixed asset register

Additional documents if applicable:

  • Previous year audit report, for comparative figures
  • Loan agreements and lease contracts
  • VAT returns, full financial year if VAT-registered
  • Payroll records, for staff cost verification
  • Shareholder agreements, for due diligence audits
