Company Formation UAE: Mainland vs Free Zone vs Offshore (2026 Guide)

Every founder researching company formation UAE eventually hits the same wall: mainland, free zone, or offshore, and which one actually fits your business?

It's not a small question. Pick the wrong structure and you might spend your first year unable to sell to the customers you actually want, or paying for a UAE presence you didn't need in the first place. Pick the right one, and company formation becomes the easiest part of building your business, not the hardest.

Here's the good news: the decision usually comes down to three or four honest questions about how your business actually works. This guide walks through everything you need to know about company formation UAE: what each structure means, how they compare side by side, and how to land on the right one without a 40-page legal brief.

Quick Answer, If You're Skimming

Structure Best For
Mainland Selling directly to UAE customers, government contracts
Free Zone International or export-focused businesses, consultants
Offshore Holding companies, investment structures, asset protection

Still not sure which row is yours? Keep reading, we'll get specific.

What Is a Mainland Company?

A mainland company is registered directly with the government: DET in Dubai, ADDED in Abu Dhabi, or SEDD in Sharjah. There's no "zone" involved. You can trade with customers anywhere on the UAE mainland.

Since the 2021 change to the UAE's Commercial Companies Law, foreigners can own 100% of most mainland businesses. A local Emirati partner is no longer required for the vast majority of activities. Only a few activities are still reserved for UAE nationals. That's a big shift from how things worked even five years ago, and it's the reason mainland has become a realistic option for far more founders than it used to be.

What mainland actually gets you:

  • Unrestricted trade anywhere across all seven emirates, with no distributor or middleman needed
  • Eligibility for government tenders and contracts: free zone and offshore companies generally can't bid on these
  • Higher credibility with UAE-based clients, who often prefer working with a locally registered, mainland-licensed entity
  • A requirement for an Ejari-registered physical address (in many cases this can be a shared workspace or flexi-desk, not a full office)

Mainland is the right call if: your customers are in the UAE, you want to work with government entities, or you simply want the freedom to trade anywhere without restriction.

What Is a Free Zone Company?

A free zone company is registered with an individual zone authority, not the federal or emirate government. Think DMCC, IFZA, JAFZA, SHAMS, DAFZA, RAKEZ, Ajman Free Zone, or Dubai South. There are more than 40 free zones across the UAE, each built around a slightly different industry focus, cost structure, and set of perks.

Free zones exist to attract foreign investment, so the pitch is simple: 100% foreign ownership as the default (not the exception), a quick setup process, and a lower entry cost than mainland in most cases.

The trade-off, and it's an important one: a free zone company is licensed to work inside its zone and internationally. To sell directly to customers on the UAE mainland, it usually needs a mainland distributor or a mainland licence. In Dubai this has eased: since 2025, Dubai free zone companies can apply to trade on the Dubai mainland through a DET licence or permit, with their free zone's approval (more on this below). If your business is built around serving local UAE customers directly, this matters. If it isn't, it barely registers.

A quick flavor of what different zones specialize in:

  • DMCC: commodities trading and professional services
  • JAFZA: logistics, manufacturing, and import/export
  • IFZA and SHAMS: cost-effective options popular with trading and media companies
  • RAKEZ: budget-friendly, broad activity list, based in Ras Al Khaima

Free zone is the right call if: your customers are primarily outside the UAE, you're a consultant or service provider serving international clients, or you want a fast, cost-effective path to a UAE entity.

What Is an Offshore Company?

Offshore is the odd one out: it's not really about operating a business in the traditional sense. Offshore companies (JAFZA Offshore and RAK ICC are the two main options) exist for holding assets, structuring investments, and international trading.

An offshore company can't lease office space, can't sponsor employee or investor visas, and can't conduct business inside the UAE. What it does offer is strong asset protection and a high degree of confidentiality. It may also benefit from tax advantages, depending on its income and the UAE Corporate Tax rules, so get tax advice before you choose it. That's why it's popular with investors who want a UAE-based holding structure for real estate, international assets, or intercompany arrangements, rather than a day-to-day operating business.

Offshore is the right call if: you're structuring investments or holdings, not running an active, customer-facing business.

Ask Yourself These Four Questions

If the table above still leaves you between two options, these four questions usually settle it:

1. Where are your customers: UAE or international?

UAE-based customers push you toward mainland. International or B2B clients make free zone the more efficient choice.

2. Do you need to bid on government contracts?

Only mainland companies are eligible. If tender work is even a possibility down the line, factor that in now.

3. Do you need to sponsor visas for yourself or employees?

Both mainland and free zone allow this. Offshore doesn't, which immediately rules it out for anyone planning to live and work from the UAE.

4. Is this an operating business, or a holding/investment structure?

Operating businesses need mainland or free zone. Pure holding structures are what offshore was built for.

Answer those four honestly, and you'll already know which row of the comparison table is yours.

Can a Free Zone Company Trade on the Mainland? Dual Licensing Explained

Here's something worth knowing before you commit: the old "free zone or mainland, pick one" rule isn't as strict as it used to be. In Dubai, Executive Council Resolution No. 11 of 2025 lets non-financial free zone companies trade on the Dubai mainland through a DET branch licence or a temporary permit, with their free zone's approval. You don't need to open a second, fully separate company.

In practice, this lets export-focused businesses keep their free zone setup and benefits while still selling directly to Dubai mainland customers when the opportunity comes up, instead of routing every local sale through a distributor. It's a recent change and each free zone applies it in its own way, so ask about it if flexibility between local and international trade matters to you. Common Mistakes to Avoid

A few patterns show up again and again with founders who end up restructuring or closing within their first year:

  • Choosing free zone purely for the lower cost, without checking whether their actual customer base is UAE-based, then needing a costly move to mainland six months in
  • Not checking visa quota limits before committing to a specific free zone, only to find they can't sponsor enough staff visas under their chosen package
  • Assuming all free zones are interchangeable. They're not. Cost, activity lists, and visa allowances vary significantly from one zone to the next
  • Overlooking the mainland office requirement until late in the process, causing delays right before license issuance

A short conversation before you file paperwork is almost always cheaper than fixing a structural mismatch after the fact.

Documents You Need for Company Formation in the UAE

The exact list depends on the authority and your activity, but most applications ask for:

  • Passport copies of all shareholders and the manager
  • A UAE visa or entry stamp, or an Emirates ID if you already live in the UAE
  • A passport-size photo of each shareholder
  • Two or three trade name options and your chosen business activities
  • Proof of address for each shareholder
  • A no-objection letter from your current employer, if you are on a UAE employment visa and the authority asks for it
  • For a company shareholder: its certificate of incorporation, memorandum and articles, and a board resolution, attested for use in the UAE

Some free zones also ask for a short business plan. We give you the exact checklist for your jurisdiction before you start, so you prepare everything once.

Corporate Tax and VAT After You Set Up

Company formation UAE doesn't end with the trade licence. Two taxes apply to most new companies, so plan for them from day one.

Corporate Tax

UAE Corporate Tax is 0% on taxable profit up to AED 375,000 and 9% above that. It applies to mainland, free zone and offshore companies. A free zone company that meets the Qualifying Free Zone Person conditions can pay 0% on its qualifying income. Every company must register for Corporate Tax with the Federal Tax Authority (FTA), even if it has no tax to pay.

VAT

You must register for VAT once your taxable supplies pass AED 375,000 in 12 months, and you can choose to register from AED 187,500. The standard VAT rate is 5%.

Accounting

Keep proper books from the start. Your Corporate Tax return, VAT returns and, for many companies, audited accounts all depend on them. Our VAT registration and filing and accounting and bookkeeping teams can take this off your plate.

Company Formation UAE: Who, What, When, Where, Why, and How

Before we get into the FAQs on structure-picking specifically, it's worth zooming out and answering the foundational questions about company formation UAE as a whole.

What is company formation in the UAE?

Company formation UAE means legally registering a business entity (mainland, free zone, or offshore) with the relevant authority so you can trade, invoice clients, sponsor visas, and open a corporate bank account under your company's name.

Who can set up a company in the UAE?

Almost anyone. UAE residents, foreign nationals living abroad, and existing international companies opening a UAE branch can all go through company formation. Since the 2021 reforms, foreign nationals can own 100% of most mainland companies, and free zones have offered full foreign ownership from day one.

When is the right time to start company formation in the UAE?

As soon as you're clear on your business activity and target market. There's no minimum waiting period or local track record required. How long the process takes depends on the authority and your activity. The real "wrong time" is starting before you've decided which structure fits, since that one decision shapes almost everything that follows.

Where can you register a company in the UAE?

Mainland companies register with DET (Dubai), ADDED (Abu Dhabi), or SEDD (Sharjah). Free zone companies register with one of more than 40 zone authorities, including DMCC, IFZA, JAFZA, SHAMS, DAFZA, RAKEZ, Ajman Free Zone, and Dubai South. Offshore companies register through JAFZA Offshore or RAK ICC.

Why do founders choose the UAE for company formation?

0% personal income tax, a 9% Corporate Tax rate that applies only to taxable profit above AED 375,000, 100% foreign ownership for most activities, clear regulation, and direct access to both the GCC region and international trade routes. That combination keeps the UAE among the top global destinations for company formation.

How does the company formation process work in the UAE?

Broadly: choose your structure and jurisdiction, reserve a trade name, submit your licence application with the required documents, receive approval, then handle Ejari or office setup, visa processing, and bank account opening. The timeline depends on the authority and your activity. We confirm yours in writing before you start.
