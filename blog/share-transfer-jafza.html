Share Transfer in JAFZA: Steps, Documents and Common Mistakes

By Modest Consulting Team Reviewed by Thekke Mannur Samuel Jose, Founder · Published November 2026 · Updated November 2026

A JAFZA share transfer moves ownership of an FZE or FZCO from one shareholder to another. You apply through the Dubai Trade Portal under Registration Amendment, upload resolutions, the corporate action form, the amended Memorandum of Association and the buyer's documents, and JAFZA's Registrar reviews them. Corporate buyers need attested papers.

This guide follows the official JAFZA guide to transfer of shares.

Can you transfer shares in a JAFZA company?

Yes. JAFZA allows shares in a company with share capital to be transferred from one party to another, for both FZE and FZCO entities. The transfer takes effect only after JAFZA approves it, with an amended Memorandum of Association and, where capital has been deposited, a new share certificate.

FZE and FZCO companies use separate forms. Check which one your company is before you start.

Do you need JAFZA approval to transfer shares?

Yes. You must file the share transfer through the Dubai Trade Portal and wait for JAFZA's Registrar to approve it. Ownership on JAFZA's records changes only after that approval, not when buyer and seller sign. The Registrar can also ask for extra documents before approving.

The portal needs a company administrator with Dubai Trade access. If your company does not have one, set that up first.

Is a JAFZA share transfer different for an FZE and an FZCO?

Yes. While an FZE and an FZCO follow the same Dubai Trade Portal steps, each uses its own corporate action form and company letterhead. An FZE has one shareholder, so a transfer changes the sole owner, whereas an FZCO has multiple shareholders and allows partial or full transfers.

Item FZE FZCO
Shareholders One shareholder More than one shareholder
Where you apply Dubai Trade Portal, Registration, "Registration Amendment - Approval", then Share Transfer Dubai Trade Portal, Registration, "Registration Amendment - Approval", then Share Transfer
Resolutions Buyer and seller resolutions (Form 10R) on FZE letterhead Buyer and seller resolutions (Form 10R) on FZCO letterhead
Corporate action form FZE version FZCO version
Share transfer and conversion are different procedures: A share transfer changes the shareholder. A conversion changes the company's legal status. An FZE has one shareholder, so to add partners the FZE must go through a conversion into an FZCO, not a share transfer.

Table source: JAFZA, transfer of shares.

What documents do you need for a JAFZA share transfer?

You need buyer and seller resolutions, the corporate action form, the signed amended Memorandum of Association, and original share certificates where capital was deposited. New individual shareholders submit identity papers, while corporate buyers must provide notarised, UAE Embassy-attested, and MOFA-attested company documents.

Documents Required Checklist

From the existing company (all transfers)
  • Buyer share transfer resolution (Form 10R), on company letterhead
  • Seller share transfer resolution (Form 10R), on company letterhead
  • Corporate action form for share transfer (FZE or FZCO version)
  • Original share certificates, if share capital has been deposited
  • Amended Memorandum of Association, signed by the shareholders
New shareholder: individual
  • Founder's details (CV)
  • Specimen signature
  • Passport copy
New shareholder: corporate
  • Certificate of registration or formation
  • Certificate of good standing
  • Memorandum and Articles of Association
  • Board resolution approving the investment in the existing company
  • Power of attorney
  • Passport copy and specimen signature of the power of attorney holder
  • Ultimate Beneficial Owner (UBO) form
  • Incumbency certificate

For a corporate buyer, the certificate of registration, certificate of good standing, Memorandum and Articles, board resolution and power of attorney must be notarised, attested by the UAE Embassy in the country of origin, and then attested by the UAE Ministry of Foreign Affairs (MOFA) once in the UAE. Drafting the amended Memorandum is legal work, which Modest coordinates through registered UAE partners.

What is the step-by-step process for a JAFZA share transfer?

The process has three stages on the Dubai Trade Portal: log in, select Registration Amendment Approval, then select Share Transfer and upload the documents. JAFZA's Registrar reviews the file. Each stage depends on complete, signed documents, and the Registrar may ask for more before approving.

Step-by-Step Process

  1. Prepare the documents. Collect the resolutions, the corporate action form and the buyer's documents. Have the amended Memorandum signed by the shareholders.
  2. Complete attestation for corporate buyers. Foreign company papers must be notarised, attested by the UAE Embassy, and stamped by UAE MOFA before upload. Modest arranges the attestation for you.
  3. Log in to the Dubai Trade Portal. A company administrator with portal access does this.
  4. Open the amendment service. Go to Registration, then choose "Registration Amendment - Approval".
  5. Select Share Transfer and upload the documents. Submit the full set in one application.
  6. Wait for the Registrar's review. JAFZA approves the transfer or asks for more documents. Reply promptly with any missing items.
  7. Receive the outputs. After approval, you get the amended Memorandum and, where capital has been deposited, a new share certificate.
After JAFZA approves the transfer: Update your company's UAE corporate bank account KYC records, notify the Federal Tax Authority (FTA) portal if your company is registered for Corporate Tax or VAT, and ensure your UBO details match the new ownership. Delaying bank KYC updates can lead to temporary account restrictions.

What affects the cost of a JAFZA share transfer?

JAFZA publishes its own official fees, so Modest does not quote amounts here. Your total depends on the company type (FZE or FZCO), whether the buyer is an individual or a company, attestation and translation of foreign documents, the number of shareholders involved, and any extra documents the Registrar requests.

Typical cost components to plan for include:

  • JAFZA Registration Amendment portal fees
  • New share certificate issuance fee (where share capital has been deposited)
  • Legal drafting of the amended Memorandum of Association
  • Notarisation, UAE Embassy and MOFA attestation, and legal translation (for foreign corporate buyers)

How long does a JAFZA share transfer take?

There is no fixed duration. The time depends on how complete your documents are, whether foreign papers are already attested, and whether the Registrar asks for more information. A complete, correctly signed file moves fastest. A missing signature or attestation sends the application back for correction.

Why are JAFZA share transfers rejected or sent back?

Most rejections come from missing, unsigned or wrongly formatted documents. Common causes are resolutions not on company letterhead, the wrong version of the corporate action form, an unsigned amended Memorandum, and foreign company papers that are not notarised and attested. Fixing these before you apply avoids repeat submissions.

Common Mistakes and Rejection Reasons

  • Buyer or seller resolution not on the FZE or FZCO letterhead.
  • Using the FZE form for an FZCO, or the FZCO form for an FZE.
  • Amended Memorandum not signed by all the shareholders.
  • Original share certificates missing when capital has been deposited.
  • Corporate buyer papers missing notarisation, UAE Embassy attestation, or UAE MOFA attestation.
  • Power of attorney missing, or the holder's passport and specimen signature missing.
  • UBO form or incumbency certificate missing for a corporate buyer.
  • No company administrator with Dubai Trade access to submit the file.
  • Not replying to the Registrar's request for more documents.
