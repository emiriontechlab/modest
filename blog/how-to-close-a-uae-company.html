How to Close a UAE Company in 2026: Liquidation, Tax, and Visas Explained

By Modest Consulting Services Reviewed by Sahal · Published May 1, 2026 · Updated May 1, 2026

In this article

  • The three tracks
  • Why dormant companies get fined
  • Which exit route fits your business
  • Step-by-step closure checklist
  • FAQs

Closing a business in the UAE is not just a single step. Many business owners think that if they stop working and cancel their office rent, their company is closed. But that is not true!

If you just walk away without following the legal rules, you might get big government fines even after two years. To close a UAE company safely, you must work on three different steps at the exact same time. For additional guidance on managing company structures before liquidation, you can also read about Transferring Company Shares in UAE.

The Three Tracks of Closing a Business

To completely shut down a business in the UAE, you need to clear three main paths:

Track What It Covers Who Manages It
Corporate Track Canceling the official business trade license Mainland authority or free zone
Tax Track Corporate tax & VAT deregistration Federal Tax Authority (FTA)
Immigration Track Cancel all employee & investor visas and establishment card MOHRE / ICP / GDRFA

If you ignore any one of these three tracks, your entire company closing process will stop!

Why "Dormant" Companies Face Big Fines

A dormant company is a business that stopped trading but never officially canceled its licenses.

Until the Federal Tax Authority (FTA) approves your tax deregistration, you are still considered an active taxpayer. This means:

  • You must still submit regular tax reports, even if your business earned $0.
  • If you do not submit these zero-value tax reports on time, you will get silent late-filing fines.
  • You must apply for VAT deregistration within 20 business days from when you stop trading.

The best rule to follow: start talking to tax experts on day one, not after canceling your license.

Which Exit Route Fits Your Business?

Every business closes for different reasons. Here are the common ways to close a company in the UAE:

Voluntary Liquidation

This route is for solvent companies that have enough money to pay off all debts, worker salaries, and government fees. A liquidator is appointed to complete a final financial report.

Strike-off or Deregistration

A faster and lighter option for small or inactive entities that have zero assets, zero debts, and no active staff.

License Freeze

This temporarily pauses your business license (usually for 1 year). It is a pause button, not a permanent closure.

Compulsory Liquidation

Used when a company cannot pay its debts and owes money to creditors. This involves legal court processes.

Step-by-Step Closure Checklist

Follow this simple order to save weeks of waiting time:

Step Action Details
1 Check Your Records Review all unpaid fines, bank accounts, staff salaries, and open tax filings.
2 Pass Resolution & Hire Liquidator Pass an official shareholder document agreeing to close the company.
3 Clear Your Tax Account Pay any remaining tax, submit final tax returns, and apply for FTA deregistration.
4 Cancel Visas Pay all final employee wages and cancel all staff and investor visas.
5 Close Bank Accounts Transfer remaining funds and close all corporate business accounts.
6 Final License Cancellation Submit all clearance papers to the licensing authority to remove your company name permanently.
