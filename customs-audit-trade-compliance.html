Customs compliance explained

What are the key customs compliance requirements for UAE businesses?

01

HS code classification

Every product imported or exported must be classified under the correct Harmonised System code. The HS code determines the duty rate. Incorrect classification results in duty underpayment — attracting FCA penalties — or overpayment — money left on the table that can often be recovered.

02

Customs valuation

UAE Customs uses the WTO Customs Valuation Agreement to determine taxable value — generally the transaction value plus additional costs. Incorrect valuation is one of the most common areas of customs non-compliance and the most common reason for FCA audit selection.

03

Duty rates and preferential rates

UAE applies 5% standard customs duty on most goods under the GCC Common External Tariff. Some goods are exempt. Preferential duty rates apply under UAE free trade agreements — many businesses miss preferential rate opportunities or incorrectly claim them without correct certificates of origin.

04

Free zone customs procedures

Goods moving between UAE mainland and free zones are subject to specific customs procedures. Incorrect handling of these movements is a common compliance risk for businesses operating across both jurisdictions.

Documents required

What documents do you need for a customs audit in UAE?

Standard documents:

  • Trade license copy
  • Import and export declarations — last 12–24 months
  • Commercial invoices — all declared shipments in review period
  • Packing lists — all shipments
  • Bills of lading or airway bills
  • Customs duty payment receipts

Additional documents if applicable:

  • Free zone gate passes — for goods through UAE free zones
  • Certificates of origin — for preferential duty claims
  • Letter of credit — for valuation review
  • Previous FCA correspondence — if responding to existing query
  • Product technical specifications — for HS code classification of complex products
